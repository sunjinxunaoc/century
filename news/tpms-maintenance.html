TPMS Valve Maintenance: How to Extend Sensor Life

Published: July 25, 2026 By Century Auto Parts

TPMS sensors monitor tyre pressure in real time and help prevent accidents caused by under-inflated tyres. A little maintenance goes a long way. These tips will help fleets and workshops get the longest life from TPMS systems.

Understand battery life

TPMS sensors run on internal batteries designed for roughly 5-10 years of service. When a low-battery alert appears, the sensor should be replaced rather than recharged - the unit is sealed for the sensor's lifetime.

Take care during tyre service

  • Use TPMS-compatible tyre changers to avoid damaging the sensor body during mounting.
  • Always replace the valve core and seal when reinstalling a sensor.
  • Check torque on the sensor nut to the manufacturer's specification - over-tightening damages the sensor.

Choose quality replacement parts

The most common TPMS failure is a leaking valve core or seal, not the electronics. Using a precision-machined, corrosion-resistant replacement valve core prevents slow leaks and inaccurate pressure readings. See our TPMS range.

Plan sensor replacement

Because all sensors in a set age at the same rate, replacing the full set at once avoids repeated service visits.
